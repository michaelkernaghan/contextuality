# Peres-33 and the Basis-Count Record

## Summary

The [[peres-33-3d]] set (33 vectors, 16 bases) held the record for the smallest known KS set in 3 dimensions since its discovery by Asher Peres in 1991. Its coordinates come from the ring Z[sqrt(-2)], placing it in the Heegner-7 [[algebraic-islands|algebraic island]].

In 2025, Cabello constructed a new 33-vector set with only 14 bases ([[cabello-2025-simplest-ks]]), breaking the basis-count record while matching the vector count. This demonstrates that the Peres construction, while historically important, is not basis-optimal.

## Key Points

- Peres-33: 33 vectors, 16 bases (1991)
- Cabello-33: 33 vectors, 14 bases (2025) — new record
- Both have 33 vectors, but Cabello's needs fewer measurement contexts
- The lower bound on vectors remains 24 ([[li-2024-sat-ks]])
- The [[kochen-specker-theorem]] guarantees no classical model exists for either set
