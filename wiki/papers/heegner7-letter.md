# New Kochen-Specker Sets from Algebraic Number Fields: the Heegner-7 and Golden Ratio Constructions (Kernaghan, 2026)

## Summary

This PRL-format letter reports two previously unknown [[ks-set|KS sets]] in dimension 3 arising from algebraic number fields not previously explored in the KS literature. The first is a 43-vector set from the Heegner-7 ring Z[(1+sqrt(-7))/2], whose orthogonality graph is non-isomorphic to all known algebraic KS sets in dimension 3. The second is a 52-vector set from the golden ratio field Q(sqrt(5)), which cannot be found by direct alphabet search and is revealed only by Hermitian orthogonal completion (cross-product closure introduces the reciprocal 1/phi with |1/phi|^2 < 1, enabling modulus-2-type cancellations absent from the raw alphabet).

The key structural finding concerns the relationship between graph-theoretic [[csw-inequality|contextual advantage]] (measured by the Lovasz theta ratio vartheta/alpha) and KS proof minimization. The Heegner-7 *full ray pool* (before minimization) achieves vartheta/alpha = 1.118, the largest among the five algebraic KS ray pools with computed CSW data. This gap is driven by *auxiliary rays* - rays that have orthogonal neighbors but participate in no designated-alphabet basis. These auxiliary rays are dispensable for KS-uncolorability (they don't appear in any basis/context) but are precisely the rays that increase the graph-theoretic contextual potential. Minimizing the KS proof removes auxiliary rays first, collapsing the vartheta/alpha gap to exactly 1.000 in the minimized 43-vector set.

The paper introduces the Hermitian orthogonal complement formula (cofactor formula) for computing the third ray completing an orthogonal pair in C^3, and demonstrates that iterating this closure can unlock KS-uncolorability where the raw alphabet alone cannot support it (the golden ratio case).

## Key Claims

- Two new KS constructions in dimension 3 (previously unreported):
  - Heegner-7: 43-vector set from Z[(1+sqrt(-7))/2], with alpha = (1+sqrt(-7))/2 satisfying |alpha|^2 = 2; found by Hermitian orthogonal completion of the 45-ray pool generated by {0,+/-1,+/-alpha,+/-alpha-bar}
  - Golden ratio: 52-vector set from Q(phi), revealed only after completion (raw alphabet {0,+/-1,+/-phi} is colorable; completion introduces 1/phi and creates modulus-2-type cancellations)
- The Heegner-7 43-vector graph is non-isomorphic to CK-31, Peres-33, Eisenstein-33, and Z[sqrt(-2)]-33 (verified by degree sequence, spectral radius, tr(A^4), and WL-1 coloring)
- CSW invariants (Table tab:csw): minimized Heegner-7 set has vartheta/alpha = 1.000; its full 145-ray pool has vartheta/alpha = 1.118, the largest among all five measured pools
- Auxiliary ray mechanism: 76 of 145 Heegner-7 pool rays (52%) are auxiliary; these add 522 orthogonal pairs and 9 hub vertices of degree 16, severely restricting independent sets; ablation study confirms removing auxiliary rays in batches smoothly degrades vartheta/alpha from 1.118 to 1.000 while preserving KS-uncolorability and all 42 bases
- Structural tension: minimizing KS proof size and maximizing graph-theoretic contextual advantage are competing objectives - the dispensable rays for the proof are exactly those that matter for the CSW advantage
- The Eisenstein pool (0% auxiliary rays) achieves vartheta/alpha = 1.074 via purely spectral effects; Peres and Z[sqrt(-2)] pools (33% auxiliary) have vartheta = alpha because their orthogonality graphs decompose into disconnected components (vartheta additive over disjoint unions)
- Both new constructions use 23 and ~25 bases respectively, making them candidates for bipartite Bell scenarios with perfect quantum strategies
- The modulus-2 cancellation pattern: the golden ratio case (|phi|^2 ~= 2.618) does not violate the pattern because completion introduces coordinates with |x|^2 <= 2

## Methods

- Hermitian orthogonal completion: cofactor formula w_k = (-1)^{k+1} det([u-bar_{k+1}, u-bar_{k+2}; v-bar_{k+1}, v-bar_{k+2}]), iterated until the ray pool stabilizes (2-4 rounds for tested alphabets)
- SAT-based KS-uncolorability testing (Glucose4 via PySAT)
- Randomized greedy deletion minimization (500 trials, seed 42)
- CSW framework analysis: independence number alpha (integer programming), Lovasz theta vartheta (SDP relaxation), fractional packing number alpha*
- Graph isomorphism verification: degree sequence, spectral radius, tr(A^4), Weisfeiler-Leman 1-stable coloring (WL-1)
- Ablation study: removing auxiliary rays in batches of 16 while measuring vartheta/alpha and verifying preserved KS-uncolorability
- Hoffman ratio H = -n*lambda_min / (lambda_max - lambda_min) as heuristic spectral indicator (not a rigorous bound for irregular graphs)
- Enumeration of three-term zero-sums from two-element alphabets {0,+/-1,+/-x}: exactly four non-trivial identities for x=2, |x|^2=2, |x|^2=x+1, x^2+x+1=0

## Relevance to Our Work

- Directly extends [[algebraic-islands-main]] by providing construction details and CSW analysis for the two new islands
- The Heegner-7 construction is one of the six [[algebraic-islands]] identified in the main paper
- The auxiliary ray mechanism introduces a new structural concept (basis-participating vs. auxiliary rays) relevant to understanding [[graph-contextuality]]
- The vartheta/alpha = 1.118 Heegner-7 pool result is the strongest graph-theoretic contextual advantage found across all six islands
- The golden ratio case demonstrates that [[algebraic-islands]] classification is properly of *completed coordinate algebras*, not raw alphabets
- The structural tension finding (proof minimization vs. CSW advantage) has implications for how [[csw-inequality]] experiments should be designed - optimal proofs may not be optimal for contextuality demonstrations
- The Hermitian cofactor completion method is used throughout all companion papers
- Cross-links to [[peres-33-3d]] (the Peres construction is one of the five pools compared in the CSW table)

## Open Questions

- Has a broader comparison of the Heegner-7 graph against full KS catalogs (using canonical labeling with nauty/Traces) been performed? The paper notes this "remains to be done"
- What is the minimum KS subset size within the Heegner-7 pool - is 43 exact, or is there a smaller subset not found by 500-trial greedy search?
- What is the minimum KS subset size within the golden ratio pool - is 52 exact?
- Do the Heegner-7 (23 bases) and golden ratio (~25 bases) KS sets actually admit new bipartite Bell scenarios with perfect quantum strategies, as conjectured?
- Is the graph-theoretic vartheta/alpha gap realized operationally - i.e., does the Heegner-7 full pool achieve actual quantum advantage in a CSW-type experiment, or only the SDP relaxation bound?
- Can the auxiliary ray mechanism be exploited deliberately to engineer high-vartheta/alpha pools from other number fields?
- Are there number fields beyond those tested that support KS sets with even larger full-pool vartheta/alpha ratios?
