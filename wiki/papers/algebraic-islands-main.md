# The Algebraic Landscape of Kochen-Specker Sets in Dimension Three (Kernaghan, 2026)

## Summary

This is the main survey paper establishing an algebraic framework for understanding which number fields can support [[kochen-specker-theorem|Kochen-Specker]] sets in dimension 3. The central finding is an empirical two-mechanism pattern: across all tested quadratic, cyclotomic, and golden-ratio fields, [[contextuality|KS-uncolorability]] arises only when the coordinate alphabet supports either *modulus-2 cancellation* (a generator x with |x|^2 = 2) or *phase cancellation* (a vanishing sum of roots of unity such as 1 + omega + omega^2 = 0). Alphabets whose generators have |x|^2 >= 3 and are not roots of unity produce orthogonal triples but not KS-uncolorability.

The paper surveys 40+ number fields and organizes KS-supporting ones into at least six discrete [[algebraic-islands|algebraic islands]]: the integer ring (CK-31), the Peres field Z[sqrt(2)] (33 vectors), the Eisenstein integers Z[omega] (33 vectors), Z[sqrt(-2)] (33 vectors), the Heegner-7 ring Z[(1+sqrt(-7))/2] (43 vectors), and the golden ratio field Q(phi) (52 vectors). Two of these - Heegner-7 and golden ratio - are reported as new KS constructions. The paper also establishes the 6|n cyclotomic theorem and provides a Jacobian-based rigidity analysis of the minimal sets.

The methodology centers on cross-product completion of coordinate alphabets drawn from algebraic number rings, followed by SAT-based KS-uncolorability testing using the Glucose4 solver. All arithmetic is exact (no floating-point). The paper provides supporting results including an OCUS-certified proof that no <=30-ray KS subset exists within the full 49-ray integer pool, universal merge saturation across all six islands, and identification of three distinct 33-vector KS sets with pairwise non-isomorphic graphs.

## Key Claims

- KS-uncolorability in two-symbol alphabets {0, +/-1, +/-x} requires either modulus-2 cancellation (|x|^2 = 2) or phase cancellation (sum of roots of unity = 0); no intermediate mechanism is observed
- Six discrete algebraic islands are identified; a seventh cubic island is confirmed at higher computational cost
- Two previously unreported KS configurations: 43-vector set from Z[(1+sqrt(-7))/2] (Heegner-7 ring) and 52-vector set from Q(phi) (golden ratio field, found only via cross-product completion)
- The cyclotomic 6|n theorem (Theorem thm:6n): the ray set S_n generated by nth roots of unity in C^3 is KS-uncolorable if and only if 6|n
- OCUS-certified proof: no KS-uncolorable subset of <=30 rays exists within the full 49-ray integer pool {0,+/-1,+/-2}^3
- Different algebraic islands yield different bipartite perfect quantum strategies and different Cabello-Severini-Winter ([[csw-inequality]]) contextual advantages; no single island dominates on all measures
- Cross-product completion can introduce cancellation identities not present in the raw alphabet (the golden ratio case: raw alphabet is colorable, completed pool is not)
- Jacobian-based rigidity analysis confirms four of six minimal KS sets are rigid in C^3
- Three distinct 33-vector KS sets with pairwise non-isomorphic graphs are identified
- The Peres and Z[sqrt(-2)] minimal KS sets are graph-isomorphic (same 33-vertex graph type)

## Methods

- Coordinate alphabet construction from algebraic number fields (quadratic, cyclotomic, golden-ratio, cubic)
- Cross-product completion: iterative closure under v x w for orthogonal pairs, until stabilization
- Exact arithmetic: Python integers, tuples (a,b) -> a + b*sqrt(d) for quadratic fields, exponent arithmetic for cyclotomic fields
- SAT-based KS-uncolorability testing: Boolean encoding with exactly-one constraints per triad, Glucose4 solver via PySAT
- Randomized greedy minimization: 200-1000 trials per configuration, random removal
- OCUS (Optimal Constrained Unsatisfiable Subset) for exhaustive certification of pool minima
- Realizability testing via L-BFGS-B numerical optimization with 50 random restarts
- Jacobian-based rigidity analysis for KS sets in C^3
- Graph isomorphism comparisons using degree sequence, spectral radius, tr(A^4), and Weisfeiler-Leman coloring
- CSW framework analysis: Lovasz theta number and independence number computation for all six islands

## Open Questions

- Does the two-mechanism pattern (modulus-2 or phase cancellation) extend to all number fields, or is there a third mechanism in some unexplored higher-degree extension?
- Is cross-product completion termination provable for specific families of coordinate rings, or only observed empirically?
- Does a KS set with fewer than 31 rays exist in any number field? (The 24-31 gap remains open)
- Whether the island classification depends on the choice of closure operation (cross-product completion vs. other closures)
- Are the Heegner-7 (43-vector) and golden ratio (52-vector) sets truly new, or do they appear in existing KS catalogs under different coordinates?
- Do the two new KS sets yield new bipartite Bell scenarios with perfect quantum strategies?
- What is the exact minimum KS subset size within the Heegner-7 and golden ratio pools (OCUS certification not yet done for these)?
