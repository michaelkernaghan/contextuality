## 2. “Note Added in Revision” — Major Foundational Issues

### 2.1. Overstatements and Unjustified Claims

#### Overclaim: “Precisely the rings whose orthogonality hypergraphs generate non-trivial pushouts…”

There is **no proof** provided (nor, as far as I can tell, in the parent paper) that the “algebraic islands” classified in Kernaghan’s paper are “precisely the rings whose orthogonality hypergraphs generate non-trivial pushouts in the sense of~[Gunji et al.]”

In fact, this statement is almost *certainly false or at least highly nontrivial*:  
- The Gunji et al. work addresses **general categorical/canonical construction of orthomodular posets and their relation to presheaf contextuality and the absence of global sections**.  
- The algebraic "island" classification in the parent paper is about which *coordinate rings/number fields* admit KS-uncolorable vector assignments in d=3. These are highly concrete, number-theoretic objects; the translation to a statement about categorical pushouts is **not constructed or even sketched** in either work.
- “Orthogonality hypergraph” is never formally defined in categorical, pushout-theoretic terms in the parent paper, nor is there any map to the Gunji et al. construction.  
- There is a categorical **analogy** between “non-trivial pushouts” gluing logics and “uncolorability contexts”, but to claim *precisely* that the six islands *are exactly* characterized as such is unwarranted without a proof or, at minimum, a lemma or explicit construction.

**Conclusion:** This paragraph’s content is **hand-waving**. No reader can check its truth from what is written, nor does the parent paper’s main body contain the required groundwork.

---

#### Misleading Presentation and Attributions

- **Gunji et al. do not prove a result about number fields or coordinate rings per se**. Their results are about categorical structures of logics and the relation of Boolean fragments under pushouts yielding orthomodularities.
- To suggest their paper “explains” *which* coordinate rings admit contextuality or defines “precisely” the algebraic islands is deeply misleading. They address the logic/categorical level, not coordinate algebra.
- The sentence, “their result provides a category-theoretic explanation for *why* contextuality forces non-Boolean structure, complementing *which* coordinate algebras support KS-uncolorability…” is rhetorically clever but methodologically imprecise and immodestly overreaching.

---

#### Use of Technical Terms and Imprecise Notation

- The phrase “orthogonality hypergraphs generate non-trivial pushouts in the sense of~[Gunji et al.]” is **undefined** — the phrase “in the sense of” masks a conceptual gap. Gunji et al. construct partial Boolean frames glued via pushouts, but how this relates to the parent paper’s algebraic structures is not spelled out.
- What is a “non-trivial” pushout, exactly? Is the failure to be distributive/Boolean the criterion? What map is being taken, among which objects? The note must either formalize this or remove the claim.
- The reference to “absence of global sections in the Abramsky–Brandenburger sheaf-theoretic framework” is correct in spirit but imprecise: the sheaf-theoretic framework is more general, and not every pushout/failure-of-global-section reduces to the specific algebraic islands found in Kernaghan’s work.
