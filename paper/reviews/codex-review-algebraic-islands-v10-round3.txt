**Claims independently confirmed**

1. **The finite reduction is valid.**

   There are
   \[
   8+32+88=128
   \]
   nonzero integer coefficient vectors of \(\ell^1\)-norm at most three. They cover every possible inner product.

   A nonzero minor is a monomial, twice a monomial, or one of
   \[
   \pm(1\pm x),\qquad \pm(1\pm x^2),\qquad \pm x(1\pm x).
   \]
   Thus its only possible nonzero roots are \(\pm1,\pm i\). These exceptions genuinely occur: the minors of \((1,x,0)\) against \((x,1,0)\) and \((x,-1,0)\) include \(1-x^2\) and \(-1-x^2\).

   Away from these exceptions, the generic ray count can also be obtained without the script:
   \[
   3\ \text{axis rays}
   +3\cdot6\ \text{two-coordinate rays}
   +(4+6\cdot4)\ \text{three-coordinate rays}
   =49.
   \]
   The counts at \(\pm1\) and \(\pm i\) are respectively 13 and 31.

   For \(x=a+ib\), the two equations
   \[
   (c_1-c_2)b=0,\qquad
   c_0+(c_1+c_2)a+c_3(a^2+b^2)=0
   \]
   give exactly the claimed types of loci. An independent SymPy calculation of real roots and pairwise intersections reproduced the nine curves and all special points. Among the 128 vectors, 24 have empty loci, 86 have finite point loci, and 18 have curve loci.

   The nine curves are precisely
   \[
   b=0;\quad a=0,\pm\tfrac12;\quad
   a^2+b^2=\tfrac12,1,2;\quad
   (a\pm1)^2+b^2=1.
   \]

   The 27 nonzero special points comprise **14 real points**
   \[
   \pm1,\ \pm2,\ \pm\tfrac12,\ \pm\sqrt2,\ \pm1/\sqrt2,\
   \pm\varphi,\ \pm1/\varphi
   \]
   and **13 upper-half-plane representatives**
   \[
   i,\ i\sqrt2,\ i/\sqrt2,\quad
   \pm1+i,\quad \frac{\pm1+i}{2},\quad
   \frac{\pm1+i\sqrt7}{2},\quad
   \frac{\pm1+i\sqrt7}{4},\quad
   \frac{\pm1+i\sqrt3}{2}.
   \]

   This includes tangencies. For example, the circles centred at \(\pm1\), both of radius one, meet tangentially at \(0\); the script finds that point and subsequently excludes it from the nonzero domain.

   Once proportionality and the vanishing coefficient vectors are fixed, the labelled quotient graph is fixed. Disconnected portions of a curve therefore need no separate cases. Conjugation preserves Hermitian orthogonality, so discarding the sign of \(b\) is legitimate.

2. **The script implements that reduction correctly on every input it actually generates.**

   In [ks_two_symbol_theorem.py](</C:/Users/Michael Kernaghan/contextuality/ks_two_symbol_theorem.py:134>):

   - `locus()` handles the linear, quadratic, negative-discriminant, zero-radius and whole-real-axis cases correctly.
   - `intersect()` handles real-axis/curve, vertical-line/circle and circle/circle intersections, including equality in its tangency tests. Distinct vertical lines and distinct concentric circles have no intersections.
   - `sample()` accepts a point only when its complete vanishing-vector set equals the set prescribed by that curve. All nine samples passed.
   - `Pt.vanishes()` and `Pt.poly_vanishes()` matched independent symbolic evaluation for every coefficient vector and possible minor at all 37 samples.

   The arithmetic is straightforward: for \(a=p+q\sqrt d\),
   \[
   a^2=p^2+q^2d+2pq\sqrt d,
   \]
   while \(x^2=a^2-B+2ia\sqrt B\). The implemented rational and radical coefficients agree with these formulas.

   There is one harmless-to-this-run limitation: `Pt` assumes canonical radical representations supplied by its helpers. Arbitrary construction such as `Pt(0,1,1)` violates that assumption. Canonicalizing or rejecting such inputs would make the class safer.

   `inner_vec()` uses the correct conjugation convention: powers \((0,1)\) contribute \(x\), and \((1,0)\) contribute \(x^*\). Orthogonality survives replacing representatives because
   \[
   \langle\lambda u,\mu v\rangle=\bar\lambda\mu\langle u,v\rangle.
   \]
   The triangle enumeration includes every triangle exactly once; in positive-definite \(\mathbb C^3\), these are precisely the triads.

   `certify3()` is sound **in this invocation**. It uses the default SAT-checked growth procedure, and every resulting colourable set supplies a valid complement-hitting constraint. All 12 uncolourable representatives recertified successfully; none ended with a budget-exhausted upper bound.

4. **Parts (i), (ii) and (iii) say what the computation establishes.**

   Part (i) lists **16 actual parameter values**, represented by 12 cases after conjugation: four integer cases, four real square-root cases, four imaginary square-root cases, and four primitive third/sixth roots. There are no missing uncolourable curves or exceptional points.

   Independently enumerating minimum subsets gave:

   - Six at \(x=2\), all isomorphic to CK-31 and forming one signed-coordinate-permutation orbit.
   - One at each of \(x=\sqrt2\) and \(x=\sqrt{-2}\).
   - Two at a primitive sixth root, both isomorphic to the named Eisenstein 33-ray graph.

   The six-set statement transfers to all four integer parameters because
   \[
   S(2)=S(-2)=S(1/2)=S(-1/2)
   \]
   as actual sets of rays.

   Part (iii) is complete. If exactly one generator is nonzero, or \(a=\pm b\ne0\), rescaling representatives gives the colourable 13-ray pool. If both vanish, there are no nonzero words. Otherwise division by \(a\) gives \(S(b/a)\).

   “Have coordinates in” naturally means **each ray admits a representative whose three entries belong to the same alphabet, in one common orthonormal coordinate system**. That is the paper’s pool definition. Making this explicit would prevent confusion with field-valued coordinates or independently chosen coordinate systems.

   The last-column caption says “the graph of the minimum set found.” One-witness VF2 testing supports exactly that statement. It would not establish uniqueness or classify all minima, but the caption makes neither claim.

6. **The Eisenstein existence statement is an easy consequence of Cabello’s published construction.**

   “The last row did not appear in earlier versions” is a statement about this manuscript, not a defensible claim of mathematical novelty.

   Let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\). Apply
   \[
   U=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\omega)
   \]
   to Cabello’s published 33-ray realization. Its three all-nonzero phase patterns transform projectively as
   \[
   (1,1,1)\mapsto(1,1,\omega),\quad
   (1,\omega,\omega^2)\mapsto(1,\omega,1),\quad
   (\omega^2,\omega,1)\mapsto(\omega,1,1).
   \]
   Signs carry through. The two-coordinate rays also admit representatives over \(\{0,\pm1,\pm\omega\}\). I explicitly checked the transformed set: **33 rays, 78 pairs, 14 triads, uncolourable**, contained in the 49-ray pool. Thus the existence portion of this row follows directly from [Cabello’s published coordinates, Eqs. (1)–(2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07335v2). The complete parameter classification and the pool-minimum certification are separate assertions.
