The substantive audit is as follows.

**Step 1: both theorems survive.**

For the \(6\mid n\) theorem, the two- and three-term vanishing-sum lemma is correct, including the alternatives obtained from the imaginary part.

- **Case 1:** Every ray receives a value. Disjoint supports permit only the axis triad. Assigning only \(e_1\) the value 1 satisfies every edge and triad.
- **Case 2:** The exclusion of all-nonzero rays from triads is valid. Two possible partners must have two-element supports; different such supports intersect in exactly one coordinate and therefore cannot be orthogonal. Equal supports would give a basis of a coordinate plane, forcing the original all-nonzero ray to vanish there—a contradiction. The matching construction colours all support-two rays, and the explicit assignment of 0 to all all-nonzero rays closes the gap in version 9. No selected rays in different support-two planes are orthogonal.
- **Case 3:** A support-two ray has only its complementary axis as a neighbour. For all-nonzero rays, the two displayed neighbours are exhaustive and complete an isolated triangle. Assigning one 1 per such triangle, one axis 1, and everything else 0 satisfies **all** constraints, including axis–support-two edges.
- **Sufficiency:** \(S_6\subseteq S_n\) is immediate when \(6\mid n\). The Cabello configuration supplies a valid uncolourable subset of \(S_6\).

I implemented the manuscript’s colourings and checked every pair and triad for all applicable \(1\le n\le30\). They passed. As an additional arithmetic check,
\[
|S_n|=n^2+3n+3,\qquad
T_n=1+\mathbf1_{2\mid n}\frac{3n}{2}
       +\mathbf1_{3\mid n}\frac{n^2}{3},
\]
which reproduces the displayed triad counts.

For the circle theorem:

1. **Linear independence is correct.** On the circle, substitution \(x=\sqrt2e^{i\theta}\) turns the five reduced monomials into distinct Fourier modes. Off the circle, independence of monomials in \(x,\bar x\) follows by the invertible substitution \(x=u+iv,\bar x=u-iv\).
2. **Special-point coincidences are handled correctly.** Every word remains nonzero on this circle. A colouring of coincident rays would pull back to a colouring of the word configuration. Universally orthogonal words cannot coincide, so its triads remain genuine triads.
3. **Part (ii) proves generic colourability in the complex plane.** This does not mean generic colourability *on the circle*. The finite union of exceptional algebraic sets is the right formulation.
4. **Part (iii) proves the stated minimum, including its consequence.** At a nonexceptional phase, all additional orthogonalities and coincidences disappear. Thus a fixed universally uncolourable list must already be uncolourable in the universal circle graph. This is not merely a lower bound on lists whose uncolourability happens to have a particular proof.

**Step 2: the replacement computations are sound within their stated domains.**

In [ks_certify_fast.py](</C:/Users/Michael Kernaghan/contextuality/ks_certify_fast.py>), selectors activate precisely the induced subset’s pair and triad constraints. The bulk growth pass checks only pair compatibility initially, but **then SAT-checks the entire grown set and falls back if necessary**. Subsequent additions are individually SAT-checked. Thus every complement clause used by the default version-10 procedure is valid.

The optional model-only growth in `ks_certify_fast3.py` is also sound under its precondition: it removes vertices until every violated pair and all-zero triad has been destroyed, without removing the seed. It is witnessed by the satisfying assignment rather than independently SAT-checked. That distinction does not invalidate the default computations.

The hitting-set cardinality bound is correctly indexed. Exhausting candidates below the current uncolourable upper bound proves minimality. Budget exhaustion is reported separately. I found no recurrence of the version-9 invalid refinement.

The census is genuinely exhaustive **at the certified minimum size**. After finding a minimum set \(S\), its blocking clause requires a vertex outside \(S\). Under the at-most-\(m\) bound, this excludes \(S\) and its proper subsets, but no other \(m\)-element set. The proper subsets are already known colourable. The reproduced numbers are:

For bipartite optimization, the monotonicity argument is correct. Inactive bases can have all their outcome variables false; their unguarded exclusion clauses therefore cause no spurious obstruction. Shared bases acquire the same choice through cross-party orthogonality. Exhausting smaller products justifies “exact” **for basis subsets of the named configuration**. [Cabello, Definition 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17735v3).
