3. **Claims independently confirmed, with calculations and methods**

**The reduction and exhaustive classification survive.**

Dividing by the smallest nonzero absolute value and sorting gives \(1<x<y\). Equal absolute values or a zero generator leave at most two nonzero symbols up to sign, so the two-symbol theorem applies.

Each inner product contains three signed monomial contributions; each minor contains two. The six possible monomials are exactly
\[
1,\ x,\ y,\ x^2,\ xy,\ y^2.
\]
The number of nonzero integer coefficient vectors with \(\ell_1\)-norm at most three is
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{3}2^j\binom6j\binom3j
=36+180+160=376.
\]
Independent enumeration agrees with `all_vectors()`. Independent factorization gives **125 factors**. `inner_vec`, `minor_vec`, `factorise`, and `norm_poly` implement this correctly; primitive normalization and positive leading coefficient remove constant and sign duplicates.

The proposed stratification is mathematically sufficient:

- Distinct irreducible factors cannot share a curve component.
- A nonvertical smooth branch meeting the open domain \(D\) has points over an open interval between the prescribed cuts. Boundary tangencies do not require additional strata inside \(D\).
- Vertical factors are handled separately.
- An isolated real point must be singular. I checked all quadratic factors: their singular zeros, where present, are at \((0,0)\), outside \(D\).

I independently checked curve existence with exact algebraic comparisons and obtained **31 curves**. I then examined all pairs of those curves, split their finite intersections using a separating coordinate \(t=x+qy\), and used rational isolating intervals to decide \(x>1\) and \(y>x\). Exact factor evaluation gave **130 special sets**, matching the archive without additions or omissions.

Thus the numerical weaknesses in the submitted enumerator have **not produced a wrong classification here**. They remain weaknesses in the claimed proof implementation.

**The certifier reductions and floor interpretation are sound.**

For a ray in no triad, extending a colouring by assigning that ray zero satisfies every incident pair constraint. Therefore deleting it preserves uncolourability. Applying this argument along the deletion sequence shows every minimum uncolourable subset lies in the computed core. A pair without a triad does not invalidate this argument.

For orbit clauses, an automorphism maps every colouring of \(M\) to a colouring of \(g(M)\). Hence every uncolourable subset must meet the complement of each image.

[`check_group`](</C:/Users/Michael Kernaghan/contextuality/ks_certify_fast4.py:61>) checks permutations and edges on the **full pool**, not triads explicitly. That is sufficient here because the triad lists are precisely all graph triangles. The core is invariant, so restricting these permutations to it is correct. I independently checked all **24 permutations in all 71 real uncolourable cases**, including triangle preservation explicitly.

The floor device remains sound: termination at the unchanged threshold \(L\) proves no uncolourable subset smaller than \(L\). If a smaller set is found, the updated value—not the original threshold—must be interpreted.

The validation records do exactly that:

- **\(2\zeta\), threshold 33:** completed with lower bound **33**, after 127 iterations.
- **\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})\), threshold 44:** found **43**, then certified **43**. This is the expected positive control, not a contradiction.
- **\(\eta/\sqrt2\), threshold 34:** the new log only records discovery of a 33-ray set. The older threshold-33 run completed and supports the paper’s minimum 33.

All **71** three-symbol records have `no_set_below_31: true`, a non-null certified value equal to the upper bound, and the expected minimum for their stratum. **Zero** contain a witness. The reason is visible in [`certify_case`](</C:/Users/Michael Kernaghan/contextuality/ks_three_symbol_real.py:373>): it retains only the greedy set’s size, and stores a witness only after an improvement.

**The family is valid, but its equivalence claims need replacement.**

The numerical results reproduce: nine sampled members have 72 pairs and one nontrivial infinitesimal flex. The trivial-motion construction is correct: nine generators of \(\mathfrak u(3)\), plus 33 individual phases, span dimension **41**, with the common scalar phase counted twice. I checked that rank at all nine samples.

At Peres’s point I also computed the Jacobian rank exactly. The real and imaginary blocks have ranks **96** and **60**, giving
\[
\dim\ker J=198-156=42,\qquad 42-41=1
\]
nontrivial infinitesimal flex.

The archived velocity calculation checks independence from trivial motions, but not the linearized constraints. Using the analytic derivatives
\[
\dot x=ix,\qquad \dot{x^*}=-ix^*
\]
repairs that check. At the three velocity samples, the constraint residual was below \(8\times10^{-17}\), and the relative distance from trivial motions was \(0.368513865595\).

I also checked the constant graph symbolically. With \(x=z/\sqrt2\), \(|z|=1\), exactly **72 inner products vanish identically**. None of the others vanishes on the unit circle, and no two rays coincide there. Thus the constant 33-ray Peres graph is established beyond sampling.

For inequivalence, the exact identities are
\[
W(\theta+\pi)=W(\theta),\qquad
P_{12}W(\theta)=W(-\theta),
\]
where \(P_{12}\) swaps coordinates 1 and 2. The latter generally relates different ray sets by a unitary.

A corrected inequivalence statement has an exact proof. For normalized representatives,
\[
\sum_{i<j}|\langle v_i,v_j\rangle|^4
=\frac{37493}{432}+\frac{17}{2}\cos(2\theta).
\]
I obtained this by symbolic summation over all 528 pairs. It is strictly decreasing on \(0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2\), proving pairwise inequivalence on that interval.

Finally, substitute
\[
a=b=1,\qquad c=\frac1{x^*},\qquad
k=-\frac{c}{c^*}=-\frac{x}{x^*}
\]
into [Gould–Aravind, Table 3](https://users.wpi.edu/~paravind/Publications/GouldPKA-FP.pdf). Their rays become exactly the archived word family, projectively and after relabelling. For example,
\[
(c^*,1,1)\sim(1,x,x),\qquad
(1,c,1)\sim(x^*,1,x^*),\qquad
(1,k,c)\sim(x^*,-x,1).
\]
I verified all 33 matches symbolically. Moreover, their general parameters are reduced to \(a=b=1\) by the diagonal unitary \(\operatorname{diag}(b,a,1)\), replacing \(c\) by \(ab^*c\). Consequently the families agree globally up to unitary equivalence. The proposed open question about agreement away from Peres is settled by this calculation.
