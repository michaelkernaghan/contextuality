**Claims independently confirmed**

- **The \(6\mid n\) proof is sound.** The two-term and three-term vanishing-sum lemma is correct. In Case 1, the axis triad is the only triad and assigning only \(e_1\) the value 1 satisfies every edge. In Case 2, the argument excluding all-nonzero rays from triads works; every ray is assigned, matching constraints are satisfied, and the selected rays in different coordinate planes cannot be orthogonal. In Case 3, each two-support ray has only its complementary axis as a neighbour, while the all-nonzero rays partition into isolated triangles. The stated assignment satisfies both kinds of constraints. Sufficiency follows from \(S_6\subseteq S_n\) and the uncolourable witness. Independently using **cyclotomic-polynomial reduction**, rather than the script’s vanishing-sum test, I checked all counts, SAT outcomes and explicit colourings for \(1\le n\le30\). Every displayed cyclotomic row matched.

- **The circle theorem’s mathematical core survives.** Fourier independence of \(1,x,x^*,x^2,x^{*2}\) on the circle is correct. Polynomial independence on the plane follows by the invertible substitution \(x=a+ib,\ x^*=a-ib\). The coincidence argument is valid: words remain nonzero, universally orthogonal words cannot coincide, and any specialized colouring pulls back. My independent reconstruction obtained:
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|rrr|c}
  &\text{vertices}&\text{pairs}&\text{triads}&\text{colourable}\\
  \text{generic plane}&145&366&22&\text{yes}\\
  \text{generic circle}&145&378&34&\text{no}\\
  W&45&98&22&\text{no}
  \end{array}
  \]
  All 22 printed triads matched. A separate implementation using fresh subset CNFs, Minisat22 and RC2 certified minimum **45**. Exact specialization gives 33 rays at both axis points. The overlap formula is correct; add the observation that finite relabellings cannot remove its continuously varying values to justify the conclusion for unlabelled sets.

- **The distinction from Gould–Aravind is sound.** Their family has 33 rays with the Peres graph. The manuscript’s specialization argument excludes sub-43 KS subsets at generic circle points because specialization to \(P(\alpha)\) is injective and preserves edges. The independently confirmed minimum 45 makes the distinction stronger. Gould–Aravind’s explicit phase family is present in their [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.4502); this does not independently certify Flores Gordillo’s later classification.

- **The new minimum certifier and census are sound under their stated preconditions.** Selector clauses activate precisely the constraints of the selected subset. The bulk growth pass may overlook triads provisionally, but its subsequent SAT check and fallback prevent that from producing an invalid hitting clause. Model-only growth removes a vertex from every violated constraint while preserving the seed. Maximality is unnecessary. Every uncolourable subset must hit each complement, so infeasibility below the known upper bound proves minimality. In `census()`, the clause blocking a found minimum set also blocks its subsets; these cannot contain another minimum set. Enumeration is therefore exhaustive. Rerunning it reproduced **6, 1, 1, 3, 6** minimum subsets for integer, Peres, imaginary Peres, Eisenstein and Heegner-7 respectively, including the reported orbit counts.

- **The BPQS model and exactness argument are correct.** It matches [Cabello’s Definition 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17735): choices are basis-dependent, exclusions cross-party, and a shared basis forces identical choices. Assignability is downward closed, so complement clauses are valid. Increasing-product search with \(p\le q\) is complete by party symmetry. I reproduced all six optima: **45, 63, 63, 72, 108 and 160**. These are exact for the specified sets and basis-subset model.

- **The CSW implementations compute the stated quantities.** MaxSAT computes independence number; the projector sum computes the attained value; the primal/dual SDPs use the correct theta convention; rational feasible LP solutions with equal objectives establish fractional-packing optimality. Edges and triangles cover maximal cliques in these three-dimensional graphs. I reran \(\alpha\), attained values and rational \(\alpha^*\) checks for all six minimum sets, and independently obtained
  \[
  \sum_{\CK}P_v=\operatorname{diag}(35/3,29/3,29/3).
  \]
  The distinction between graph theta and the value of fixed rays agrees with [CSW](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.7081).

- **The main numerical catalogue corrections hold.** I recertified the four completed integer/Peres/imaginary-Peres/Eisenstein minima as **31/33/33/33**, and the raw supergolden minimum as **47**. I reproduced the core isomorphism and both pool non-isomorphisms. There are exactly **48 core isomorphisms**, none compatible with the auxiliary-neighbourhood multisets; the raw pools also have exactly equal characteristic polynomials. I found **no numerical transcription mismatch** between the fixed printed catalogue, profiles, BPQS, CSW and rigidity rows and the corresponding stored records inspected. The completed Gaussian bound \(\le33\) remains valid by inclusion despite its standalone search recording a weaker bound.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11640). Cabello supports 33 rays, 14 bases, \(5\times9\), and unitary uniqueness. I additionally matched the stored Eisenstein witness to his construction: applying \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\omega)\) gives the union of the three \(Z\)-images of Yu–Oh.
