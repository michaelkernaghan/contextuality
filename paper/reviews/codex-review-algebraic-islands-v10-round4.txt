**3. Claims independently confirmed, with methods**

- **The six monomials, coefficient bound and 376 vectors are correct.** A product of letters, with or without conjugating the first factor, is zero or a signed member of
  \[
  1,\ x,\ x^*,\ xx^*,\ x^2,\ x^{*2}.
  \]
  An inner product has three terms; a minor has two. Independently enumerating signed monomial products gave all **376** nonzero inner-product coefficient vectors:
  \[
  \#\{\|c\|_1=1\}=12,\qquad
  \#\{\|c\|_1=2\}=72,\qquad
  \#\{\|c\|_1=3\}=292.
  \]
  Their sum is 376. The minor coefficient family is smaller; the common bound \(\|c\|_1\le3\) is nevertheless correct.

- **Both printed vanishing conditions and the stratification are correct.** Direct expansion gives
  \[
  \Im f=b[(c_1-c_2)+2(c_4-c_5)a],
  \]
  \[
  \Re f=c_0+(c_1+c_2)a+(c_3+c_4+c_5)a^2
             +(c_3-c_4-c_5)b^2.
  \]
  Put \(L(a)=(c_1-c_2)+2(c_4-c_5)a\) and write the real equation as \(p(a)+kB=0\). If \(L\not\equiv0\), a nonreal solution requires its single possible root; this produces an isolated point or a vertical line. Real solutions come from \(p(a)=0\), including the whole real axis when \(p\equiv0\). If \(L\equiv0\), the equation gives \(B=-p/k\), or vertical lines when \(k=0\).

  **No line or hyperbola branch is missed.** On a nonvertical branch with \(b\ne0\), infinitely many vanishing points force \(L\equiv0\). Substitution of \(B=g(a)\) then gives a polynomial in \(a\); infinitely many roots force it to vanish identically. It therefore vanishes on the entire prescribed curve. Also, replacing \(b\) by \(-b\) leaves both zero conditions unchanged. This deals with all three reducible pairs \(b^2=a^2,a^2/3,3a^2\), and with disconnected hyperbola branches.

- **The fourteen listed curve loci and the special-point coverage are correct.** The script produces eleven retained graphs in \((a,B)\) and three vertical lines. Its four discarded graphs are
  \[
  B=-2-a^2,\quad -1-a^2,\quad-\tfrac12-a^2,\quad-a^2;
  \]
  the first three have no real points and the last contributes only the excluded origin.

  Independent SymPy intersection and isolated-root calculations reproduced exactly the same **53 upper-half-plane representatives**. Fourteen are real; the other 39 acquire distinct conjugates:
  \[
  14+2(39)=92.
  \]
  Further sign reduction gives **28 point classes and 12 curve classes**, hence 41 cases after both reductions. The advertised 68 cases already use conjugation.

  Tangencies are included through repeated quadratic roots. Graph/vertical intersections and intersections with \(B=0\) are included. Word coincidences need not be isolated: they occur along entire loci such as the axes and unit circle. The stratification handles these through the minors; it does not need to declare every coincidence point special.

- **The arithmetic and pool-construction code are sound on the enumerated domain.** `Q2.sign()` uses rational signs and the exact comparison \(p^2-q^2d\). In its actual normalized representations, the problematic equality case cannot occur with a nonzero irrational coefficient. Floating-point approximations are used for ordering and reporting, not vanishing decisions.

  `Pt.vanishes()` correctly drops the imaginary restriction when \(B=0\). `decompose()` implements the cases above. `arrangement()` includes all relevant intersection types. Both sampling routines explicitly check that the vanishing-vector set equals the set expected for the whole curve. I additionally checked **120,320 vector/point incidences**, covering the computed strata and a rational grid, without a discrepancy.

  `inner_vec()` correctly conjugates the first word; `minor_vec()` correctly uses ordinary, unconjugated products. All formal words remain nonzero when \(x\ne0\), so the minor test is valid. At zero that premise fails, but zero is excluded from the theorem and was checked separately by direct construction.
